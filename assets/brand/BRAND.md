Porch is a private place for people who already know each other. Keep it warm and plainspoken. Use Porch in prose, porch in the drawn wordmark, and porch.chat when an address is needed. Keep the existing lines “Your people. Your place.” and “Pull up. Talk awhile.” Neither belongs inside the app icon.

The chosen direction is Evening Threshold, the refinement in concept D. It keeps the peaked porch entrance from the old identity and replaces the speech bubble with a lit doorway. The lantern in A felt too ornamental at 16 pixels. The chairs in C were appealing but the slats and shared armrest became noise. B supplied the doorway idea; D brings back the recognizable roof.

The previous porch-site icon used emerald #34D399, teal #14B8A6 and blue #2563EB in a gradient, a white roof, a white chat bubble and three colored dots. The old wordmark said Porch.chat in a heavy sans serif. The actual site had already moved toward near-black #111210, cream #F4E8D7, orange #EE4F25, mint #68D7B3 and serif headlines. We keep its cream, near-black, architectural idea and welcoming copy. We remove the gradient, dot row, bubble tail and decorative tagline from the mark. Warm amber replaces blue as the main action color; mint stays as a quieter link and status color. Old files in reference are historical comparison only, not runtime assets. No old application code is reused.

The production icon is hand-built SVG, not an image-generation crop. Its roof and posts surround one amber doorway on pine #182B25. No shadows, glow filters or gradients. Keep the icon intact; never stretch it or add a notification dot to the artwork. Prefer at least 24 pixels in navigation, with the supplied 16-pixel export reserved for favicons. Leave one post-width of clear space around the standalone mark and at least 24 pixels around a full-size lockup. Use the inverse lockup on dark surfaces, the ordinary lockup on cream, and the single-color mark for engraving or monochrome printing. The standalone light-background mark uses burnt amber for contrast. The icon keeps a dark container in both themes.

The logo wordmark is Manrope at weight 650, tightened slightly and exported as paths so installation cannot change it. Use Manrope 400 for messages and labels, 600 for controls, and 700 for headings in the app. The original variable font and its SIL Open Font License are included in fonts. Fraunces 600 is the recommended optional display face for site headlines only, not chat messages or controls. It is also SIL OFL 1.1, but is not bundled here. Sources checked for this package are https://github.com/googlefonts/manrope and https://github.com/undercasetype/Fraunces.

tokens.json and tokens.css contain matching dark and light semantic colors. Dark uses #111210 for the page, #191D19 for surfaces, #232B24 for raised surfaces, #F4E8D7 for text, #B8B5A6 for secondary text and #F3B85A for actions with #241B0F action text. Light uses #FAF5EB, #FFFCF6 and #EDE7DB for those surfaces, #182B25 and #566256 for text, and #8C4418 for actions with #FFFCF6 action text. Do not use the bright decorative amber as small text on cream.

contrast.json records computed WCAG ratios for primary text, muted text, links, semantic statuses and action labels. Those sampled pairs all exceed 4.5 to 1. This is a palette check, not a full UI accessibility audit. Pair status color with a label or icon. Keep focus rings visible and avoid pulsing lights, texture or gradients behind conversation text.

final contains SVG and PNG versions of the mark, outlined wordmark and horizontal lockup. icons contains 16, 32, 48, 64, 128, 256, 512 and 1024 pixel PNGs plus a multi-resolution Windows ICO. comparison.png places the actual old icon beside the actual new vector render. size-proof.png shows the small exports at native pixel sizes. concepts contains generated explorations, not approved production artwork.

This is a design proposal, not a trademark clearance or a product deployment. The selected geometry is deliberately simple; test recognition with the people who will use Porch before treating it as final public branding.
